18 · Tall buildings

Transfer structures and floating columns

When a column or wall above has no column below, the load must be carried sideways first. That detour is what a transfer structure does, and it comes at a cost.

A column sitting on a beam

Move the load along the beam. The closer to the middle, the more the beam bends. See how deep it has to be.

A wall that stops at the podium

Above the podium, walls make the building stiff. Below, a few columns. That stiffness cliff makes the transfer floor a soft storey. Make the podium stiffer and watch the ratio.

Check yourself

Keep in mind

  • 1Avoid transfers if you can. Try to line up walls and columns.
  • 2If you must, make the transfer structure a deep beam or thick slab designed for strut-and-tie action.
  • 3Check the soft storey at the transfer level and use shear walls to carry through.
  • 4Put extra strength in the supporting columns: they carry the whole redirected load plus overturning.
