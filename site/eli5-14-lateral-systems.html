14 · Tall buildings

How a high-rise stands up: lateral systems

A tower is a cantilever fixed in the ground. The system you pick decides how it leans, and how tall it can get before the cost explodes.

Five ways to resist the wind

Pick a system. Each bends differently: frames lean like a rack, walls like a diving board. Slide the height to see if the system is in its comfort zone.

Outriggers: tie the core to the edge

An outrigger pulls the far columns into the work. Move it up or down the building and change how stiff it is. There is a best floor.

Check yourself

Keep in mind

  • 1Use the simplest system that meets drift: frames below ~25 floors, walls to ~35–40, then add dual action, outriggers or a tube.
  • 2Outrigger levels are heavy: they often double as refuge or MEP floors.
  • 3Differential column shortening between core and perimeter loads the outrigger. Plan the construction sequence.
  • 4A hard-working core needs shear, not just bending: check the base shear stress and coupling beams.
