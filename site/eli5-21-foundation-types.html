21 · Geotechnical and foundations

Shallow versus deep foundations

A foundation is a way of spreading load into the ground. If the ground near the surface is good, spread it wide. If not, reach down to something better.

Pick a foundation for the site

Set the column load, the grid and the ground. The footings grow until they touch, then a raft is cheaper. If the soil is poor, piles reach past it.

Load spreads wide as it goes down

Under any foundation the pressure fades with depth. A small footing’s influence dies at about twice its width. A big raft reaches many metres deeper.

Check yourself

Keep in mind

  • 1Start shallow. Move to a raft, then piles, only when the soil or the loads demand it.
  • 2Differential settlement matters more than total settlement. Keep the foundation type uniform within a building.
  • 3Check the water table level and rise over the life of the building, including construction dewatering.
  • 4Tall towers usually use piled rafts: piles carry most of the load, the raft spreads the rest.
