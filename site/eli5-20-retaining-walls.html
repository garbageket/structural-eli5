Retaining walls, in pictures

Soil wants to slide down. A wall stops it. Everything else is about how hard the soil pushes, and whether the wall can take it.

With a wall, the soil leans on it, harder the deeper you go.

Soil pushes sideways, harder the deeper you go

Think of a sack of rice standing up: the bottom bulges most. The sideways push is a fraction K of the weight of soil above. Change the height, and drag along the wall to read the pressure at any depth.

Water is the real enemy

Water pushes equally in every direction, so for water K is 1, three times what dry sand gives. Raise the water table and watch the push.

So drain it

Drainage lets you design for less water, but only where the water table stays below the wall for good. In Mumbai basements the water table is usually high: design for full water pressure and uplift instead.

What adds push, and what takes it away

Heavy rollers near the wall

Compaction locks in extra push near the top. Use light plate compactors within about 1–1.5 m of the wall, in thin layers.

Swelling clay

Black cotton soil expands when wet and can push many times harder than Ka. Never use it as backfill.

Rough wall back

Wall friction δ (about 2/3 φ) tilts the push downward and trims its horizontal part a little (Coulomb).

    Value engineering

    Settle the water first

    It can double the push. Drain where the water table stays low. Below a permanent water table, design for full water pressure and uplift, and don’t pretend drains will relieve it.

    Choose the backfill

    Granular, free-draining, φ ≥ 30°, non-swelling. Going from φ 25° to 35° cuts Ka by a third. Never black cotton soil.

    Grow the heel, not the toe

    Soil sitting on the heel is free weight against sliding and overturning.

    Shear key before a wider base

    If only sliding fails, a key under the stem is cheaper than more footing.

    Taper and curtail

    Moment goes with h³. Thin the stem towards the top and stop alternate bars once the moment has halved.

    Counterforts past 6–7 m

    A cantilever stem gets too thick and heavy in steel. Ribs at about H/3 to H/2 spacing turn the stem into a slab spanning between them.

    Let floors prop basement walls

    A propped wall spanning floor to floor is far thinner than a cantilever. Design it for K0 plus water, and check the temporary stage before the slabs are cast.

    Be honest about passive

    Ignore the top metre or so in front of the toe; it can be dug out for drains or services later.

    Don’t bank on cohesion

    For permanent works use drained strength (c′, φ′) of the backfill, not short-term undrained clay strength.

    Indian codes to keep at hand

    • IS 456:2000, cl 20: stability against overturning and sliding; member design of stem, heel and toe.
    • IS 1893 (Part 3):2014: seismic design of bridges and retaining walls, dynamic earth pressure.
    • IS 14458 (Parts 1–3): guidelines for retaining walls in hill areas.
    • IS 6403: bearing capacity of shallow foundations. IS 1904: design of shallow foundations.
    • IS 3370 (Part 2): crack-width control for water-tight basement and retaining walls.
    • IS 2720 (Parts 11, 12, 13): triaxial and direct shear tests for c and φ.
