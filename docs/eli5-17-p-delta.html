P-Delta: when weight makes the lean worse

A leaning building has weight above that pulls it further over. The more it leans, the bigger the pull. This second-order effect grows quickly in tall, flexible buildings.

Lean it and watch the weight help

A cantilever column under a sideways force and a weight at the top. Raise the weight towards its buckling load and the lean runs away.

In a tower, the base storey feels it most

The stability index of a storey is the weight above times the drift, over the storey shear. At the base the weight is greatest.

Keep in mind

  • 1Include P-Delta in every tall building analysis. It is cheap and avoids unconservative drift.
  • 2If θ goes above 0.25, the structure is flirting with instability: stiffen it.
  • 3P-Delta and column slenderness are different things: P-Delta is global lean, slenderness is local bow.
  • 4Use the nominal gravity load pattern consistent with the combination, not an arbitrary one.
