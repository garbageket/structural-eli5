Piles: how they carry load

A pile holds up a building in two ways: friction along its sides and bearing at its tip. Which one does the work depends on the ground it passes through.

Skin friction and tip bearing

Choose the soil along the pile, then change its length and width. Watch how much comes from the shaft and how much from the tip.

Check yourself

Keep in mind

  • 1Rock sockets and end-bearing in hard strata give stiff piles. Friction piles in clay can settle a lot.
  • 2Do load tests on trial piles before final design. They calibrate the soil numbers.
  • 3Piles take lateral load poorly. Add raking piles or rely on basement walls and the raft.
  • 4Negative skin friction from settling fill can add to pile load: check it for fill sites.
